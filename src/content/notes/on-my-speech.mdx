Oftener than I would like doth it happen. I ask some simple thing, the road to the next village or the price of a loaf, and the one I ask looketh upon me as though I had spoken in riddles. Some think that I mock them; others take me for a player escaped from a masque, the mask and the speech together.

To say less have I learned, and to say it slowly.

Among mine own people never had I this trouble; there I speak as I breathe. 'Tis only here, in the Common Tongue, that every sentence is a small river to be forded.
