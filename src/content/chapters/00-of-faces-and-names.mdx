At last have I begun this book. Such books Master Maurevelle kept all his life, and long have I meant to keep one of mine own.

Ere the first page is filled, I would set down that which shall never be written in it. Their faces and their true names my people give only unto kin, and to write a true name where any eye may find it is, to us, a thing close to betrayal. There was an age, the Era of Records, when those who hunted us wrote down every name, every face and every tie of blood, and of that ledger made a chain for whole families. This we have not forgotten.

Thus the only name of mine in this book shall be Malrien Vealwood, the name that goeth with my mask. Of kin I shall write as kin, and of friends by the names they give unto the world. Where a true name belongs, there shall be silence alone.

## Of the Mask

Mine own mask was broken; of that shall I write when I am able. The one I now wear is an Ash Mask, which I had in Gridania. Among my people, a mask is a history: carved and painted with that which its wearer hath been and done, mended through the years and never cast aside. No signs doth this one bear as yet. 'Tis my hope that these pages may serve it for one.

## Of My Master

From him have I my speech: a scholar of Sharlayan who read old manuscripts so long that he came to speak as they do. All that he saw he wrote down, and yet in all his papers on my people there was not one true name. As a child, I understood not how great a courtesy that was.

When I returned to him with the marks of an Archon upon my neck, he was gone, and all his things with him. Where he went, or why, I know not. Upon the road I ask after him when I may, and so shall I continue.

## Of This Book

Heroes there are abroad in Eorzea, and of them shall the bards sing. This is not their tale. Smaller is that which I shall write here: the roads I walk and what I learn upon them, set down lest they fade behind me.
